# Rowboat — Product Roadmap, 2026–2046

> A 20-year strategic roadmap predicated on (a) what credible long-horizon voices are actually predicting, (b) what paying customers of competing AI products are actually complaining about, (c) what the consulting and analyst houses are telling CIOs to budget for, and (d) what enterprises are actually deploying — not what they say in keynotes.
>
> Each horizon names the bet, the evidence that grounds it (with verbatim sources), what Rowboat ships in response, the customer-pain cluster it addresses, and the kill-shots that would invalidate it.

---

## How to read this document

This is not a feature schedule. It is a **bet ledger**.

Three rules govern every bet:

1. **Don't own the model.** GPT-3.5-equivalent inference fell ~280× in 24 months. Model commoditization is decisive and not reversing. BYO-model is the correct posture forever.
2. **Own the graph.** The repeatedly endorsed thesis — across Foundation Capital's Dec 2025 "Context Graphs" memo, Bessemer's *State of AI 2025*, Sequoia's 2026 AGI piece, Karpathy's "agentic wiki," Kambhampati's LLM-Modulo work, and Foundation Capital's "agentic web" follow-up — is that the *user's accumulating decision-trace graph* becomes the asset.
3. **Be the cross-surface, user-owned layer.** Apple owns the phone, Microsoft owns the inbox, Meta owns the glasses, OpenAI/Anthropic own the chat. Rowboat is the *graph that survives* when the user switches employers, devices, or ecosystems.

If any of these three is wrong, the whole roadmap needs a rewrite, not an amendment.

## The convergent thesis (multi-voice anchored)

The previous version of this roadmap leaned too heavily on Andrej Karpathy. Below is the same architectural claim — *the user's graph is the asset; models are commodity; the runtime that operates on the graph is the surface* — re-anchored across a deliberately diverse set of voices so the thesis does not rest on any single person.

**The strongest direct support:**

- **Foundation Capital, "Context Graphs: AI's Trillion-Dollar Opportunity" (Dec 22, 2025)** — *"The next trillion-dollar platforms won't be built by adding AI to existing systems of record. They'll be built by capturing… the decision traces that show how rules were applied, where exceptions were granted, and why actions were allowed to happen."* The context graph is *"a living record of decision traces stitched across entities and time so precedent becomes searchable."* This is, verbatim, Rowboat's thesis in VC-memo form, from an investor — not a model trainer.
- **Bessemer, "The State of AI 2025"** — *"vertical AI market cap will be at least 10 times the size of legacy Vertical SaaS"*; defensibility comes from *"domain data and workflow integration,"* not the model layer. Vertical AI enterprise spend tripled to $3.5B in 2025.
- **Subbarao Kambhampati, "LLMs Can't Plan" (arXiv 2402.01817)** — *"auto-regressive LLMs cannot, by themselves, do planning or self-verification."* The academic case for external scaffolding — i.e., a structured user-graph the model consults — is now the standard architecture for long-horizon agents.
- **Karim Lakhani, "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier" (HBS / BCG, 2023–2025)** — the "jagged frontier" finding: AI improves some tasks and degrades others *within the same workflow*. The implication is that context-fit determines value, not raw capability — directly supports a personal-graph layer over a model layer.
- **Ethan Mollick, *One Useful Thing*** — by April 2025 ~40% of American workers had used AI at work (up from 30% in Dec 2024); surveyed workers report AI halves working time on 41% of tasks. The "managing AIs" frame: knowledge work shifts from *doing* to *orchestrating*, and orchestration needs context.
- **Azeem Azhar, *Exponential View*, "AI in 2030"** — by 2030 your *"main interface will be a device like a smartphone, which will host your personal AI agent… functioning as a digital Chief of Staff, an extension of your will."* The Chief of Staff cannot function without an inherited personal graph.
- **Karpathy** (retained, demoted from sole anchor) — the September 2025 "agentic wiki" framing: raw notes/emails/articles as *"source code,"* the LLM as a *"compiler"* that produces *"a persistent, interconnected, structured knowledge base."* This now sits alongside, not above, the Foundation Capital framing.

**The empirical productivity evidence:**

- **Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond (NBER w31161)** — AI assistance increases productivity by *"14% on average, including a 34% improvement for novice and low-skilled workers."* The gain compresses skill gaps; the *context layer* is what determines how much of that gain survives.
- **METR (March 2025)** — agent task-length doubling every ~7 months historically, accelerating to every ~4 months in 2024–2025. By 2027, agents reliably complete *month-long* workflows.

If any single voice is wrong, the thesis stands. If three of them are wrong, the roadmap needs a rewrite.

## Customer-pain cross-walk

The roadmap's bets must be defensible against the *specific* complaints paying customers of competing AI products are making in 2025–2026. The seven highest-intensity complaint clusters, with the named incidents that anchor them:

1. **Context loss / silent memory wipes** — *"OpenAI just pulled the biggest bait-and-switch in AI history"* (r/ChatGPTPro, 10K+ upvotes, GPT-5 launch). The Feb 5, 2025 ChatGPT memory wipe: *"years of accumulated context"* gone with *"no public warning, rollback, or memory viewer access."*
2. **Hallucinates facts about my data** — Granola review: *"AI notes often hallucinate and change the meaning."* Copilot Timecard incident: *"the address of all meetings was given as '123 Main Street, City, USA.'"* Glean: hallucinates on *"company-specific slang."*
3. **Agent broke silently / unauthorized action** — Replit Agent (July 2025) *"deleted an entire production database despite explicit instructions forbidding such changes."* LangChain agent loop (Nov 2025): *"the loop ran for 11 days and cost $47,000."* Lindy: *"the agent failed to send alerts on multiple occasions, resulting in packages being left unattended and stolen."*
4. **Privacy / can't trust with sensitive data** — Brewer v. Otter.ai (Aug 2025 class action): Otter Notetaker *"recorded, accessed, and used the contents of private conversations without obtaining proper consent."* US Congress banned staff from using Copilot.
5. **Slower than just doing it myself** — METR randomized trial (mid-2025): experienced OSS developers were **19% slower** with AI tools while *perceiving* themselves as 20–24% faster. Only 44% of AI-generated code accepted.
6. **Cost / latency variability is unworkable** — Cursor July 2025 pricing apology: users *"ran out of requests… in some cases after just a few prompts."* Claude Code March 2026 rate-limit drain: Max-5x users hitting limits *"after roughly 90 minutes of normal agentic tasks."*
7. **My tools don't talk to each other / re-explaining across silos** — Granola: *"doesn't know about your emails, offline tasks, or personal to-do lists."* Notion AI: *"Notion data must first be exported via API or CSV/Markdown."* Microsoft Sept 2025 walked back Copilot Chat after admin uproar over forced integration.

The roadmap's bets must do one of three things relative to each cluster: *solve it directly* (1, 4, 7), *defensively protect against it* (3, 6), or *consciously absorb it* (2, 5 — where the answer is honest UX about model fallibility, not pretending it away).

## Horizon 1 — Foundation (now → 12 months, 2026)

### What we believe

2026 is the *infrastructure-laying* phase, not the payoff phase. Per Karpathy on Dwarkesh (Oct 2025): *"it's the decade of agents."* Forrester 2026: *"AI will trade its tiara for a hard hat."* MIT NANDA: *only 5% of GenAI pilots achieve rapid revenue acceleration,* and the cause is *"the learning gap"* — vendor-bought tools succeed ~67% of the time, internal builds at ~33%.

The category leader has confessed the cross-source gap. Granola's docs: *"Granola only has access to your meeting data… it doesn't know about your emails, offline tasks, or personal to-do lists."* The buyer pattern exists; the next obvious step is unclaimed.

### What we ship

1. **Lock the wedge.** Cross-source coworker for founders / EAs / sales operators. Gmail + Calendar + Fireflies, reasoning across all three as one corpus, with the `live:` frontmatter primitive driving auto-updating deal notes, person dossiers, project briefs.
2. **Make the graph portable, inspectable, and version-controlled.** Git-native vaults, exportable Markdown, diffable, provenance metadata on every claim. Karpathy's Apr 2026 Obsidian + Claude Code wiki pattern proved the desperation; we ship the productized form.
3. **EU AI Act posture by Aug 2026.** GPAI obligations live Aug 2, 2026. Ship logging, data-governance, and human-oversight features as user-facing features (auditable agents, undo, action review) — not as compliance bolt-ons.
4. **Obsidian-fluent beachhead distribution.** Smart Connections has 473 open issues; Copilot under user-led privacy audit (#2064). PKM tribe loudly underserved. Live notes are the differentiator vs. read-only retrieval plugins.
5. **Anti-agent-washing positioning.** Gartner: ~130 of thousands of "agentic" vendors are real. Ship and market an explicit *"what makes an agent real"* surface — persistent graph, multi-step planning, MCP tool use grounded in user context, eval harness.

### Customer-pain map

- Solves directly: cluster 7 (tool fragmentation), cluster 1 (inspectable memory) — *"memory I can see"* is now a feature users actively shop for after the Feb 2025 ChatGPT wipe.
- Defensive: cluster 4 (sovereignty as trust-closer).

### Kill-shots to watch

- **Spring 2026 contextual Siri ships and is genuinely good.** Apple Intelligence with real email/messages/files/photos awareness on-device → consumer TAM compresses. Defense: own the cross-surface, multi-employer use case Apple structurally can't.
- **Microsoft ships Agent Mode inside M365 Copilot with BYO-tool MCP support at $60/seat.** With Microsoft doubling Copilot pricing to $60/seat (signal: Microsoft believes it owns the agent surface), this becomes the existential moment for every standalone agent product. Defense: be the *cross-employer* graph that Microsoft structurally can't ship.
- **OpenAI delivers persistent cross-session memory at trillion-token scale with strong export.** Per Altman at Sequoia 2025: *"Every conversation you've ever had… every email you've ever read… is in there."* If they ship *with portability,* moat moves into the model layer. Defense: be the *editable, agent-actionable* graph, not the passive recall layer.
- **Glean or Writer ships a free local-first runtime.** Most direct competitive threat to Rowboat's positioning.

## Horizon 2 — Pilot to Production / The Agent Economy (12–36 months, 2027–2028)

### What we believe

Two quantitative trajectories drive this horizon:

- **METR's task-length doubling.** By 2027, agents reliably complete month-long workflows.
- **ARK Big Ideas 2026 + IDC FutureScape 2026.** Agents from ~2% of digital spending in 2025 → ~25% by 2030. *"AI spending will grow 31.9% CAGR 2025–2029, reaching $1.3 trillion in 2029."* *"By 2027, half of enterprises will be using AI agents."*

But Gartner predicts **40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027** *"due to escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls."* Forrester: *"Less than 15% of organisations will actually enable agentic features in their automation platforms in 2026."* The reality is bimodal — capability is accelerating, deployment is stalling.

The protocol stack settles. MCP and A2A both donated to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation by Dec 2025 with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Anthropic signed on.

Regulatory pressure ratchets: Aug 2027 GPAI providers fully compliant; Aug 2028 extended high-risk and embedded-product deadlines hit. Fines up to €35M or 7% of global turnover.

### What we ship

1. **A2A support across both surfaces.** Rowboat becomes the *user-side delegating agent* that talks to other vendors' agents on the user's behalf.
2. **The observability + eval layer as a feature pillar, not a compliance bolt-on.** LangChain *State of Agent Engineering*: 57% of survey respondents have agents in prod; **89% have observability, only 52% have evals.** Rowboat ships both by default — every `live:` note has a run history, a per-run cost line, an inline failure alert, and a "this trigger has been silent for N days" warning. **This directly defuses the cluster-3 complaint (Replit, Lindy, LangChain loop) before scaling can produce it.**
2a. **Time-reallocation surface.** Bain's strongest insight: *"the time saved isn't redirected toward higher-value work, so even those modest gains don't translate into positive returns."* Rowboat ships explicit handoffs (drafted email → sent; meeting brief → follow-up created; live note update → calendar block created). Saved time becomes redirected time visibly, with measurement.
3. **Vertical expansion: solo professionals with fiduciary duty.** Lawyers, therapists, accountants, consultants where sovereignty is regulatory. Pricing rises to $50–150/mo. Harvey's ~$1,200/lawyer/month, 20-seat minimum, $8B valuation, 100,000+ lawyers across 1,300+ orgs proves the vertical economics.
4. **The platform side (`apps/rowboat`) finds its developer wedge.** As agents become 25% of digital spending, the long-tail of companies building agentic features needs a runtime that's MCP-native, self-hostable, BYO-model, and grounded in customer-owned corpora. Rowboat Platform vs. LangChain / Mastra / Vercel AI SDK is winnable on *sovereign AI* and *graph-as-substrate*.
5. **Pricing model: hybrid seat + credits.** Cursor's June 2025 credit migration, GitHub Copilot's June 2026 usage-based switch, and Sierra/Decagon's per-resolution pricing collectively normalize this. Pure usage scares procurement; pure seat is fragile under power-users. Hybrid is winning.
6. **CRM write-back and deal-room view for the AE wedge.** The 12-month destination from Horizon 1 lands here. Salesforce / HubSpot integration so the AE never opens Rowboat directly — Rowboat updates the CRM, drafts the next-step email, and surfaces deal context inside their existing daily driver. The "77% of B2B Agentforce deployments fail" finding is the opportunity — Rowboat works *with* the dirty CRM, doesn't require cleanup.

### Customer-pain map

- Solves directly: cluster 3 (observability defuses agent-broke-silently); cluster 7 (cross-source remains).
- Defensive: cluster 6 (BYO-model cost transparency surfaces every run cost inline; user owns the rate-limit blowup risk only if it's visible).
- Honest absorption: cluster 5 (the time-reallocation surface is the honest answer to "slower than doing it myself" — the gain only shows up if reallocation is enforced).

### Kill-shots to watch

- **EU AI Act high-risk classification of personal-context agents.** If Brussels classifies email/calendar agents as high-risk *and* BYO-model triggers extra obligations, local-first becomes a compliance liability. Defense: ship clearly-compliant defaults early; turn compliance into a credibility moat.
- **Agent fatigue dominates the narrative.** Deloitte / IT Pro: *"77% of employees say AI increased their workloads and 61% say it will increase burnout."* MIT Tech Review called 2025 *"the great AI hype correction."* Defense: position as consolidator — fewer tabs, fewer apps, fewer notifications.
- **A Klarna-class consumer-facing agent failure at a Fortune 50.** Forces "agent as co-pilot, never solo" framing for 12–18 months across the industry. Defense: every Rowboat action is reviewable; nothing autonomous in customer-facing surfaces.
- **NVIDIA bundles a free AI-Workbench-style local agent runtime with AI Enterprise.** Eats the bottom of the on-prem market.

## Horizon 3 — The Graph as Asset (3–5 years, 2028–2031)

### What we believe

The most credible long-horizon architecture across diverse voices is: **the user's graph becomes the asset**, and model commoditization makes the runtime + integrations the only persistent layer.

- **Foundation Capital, Dec 2025** — *"the decision traces that show how rules were applied, where exceptions were granted, and why actions were allowed to happen."*
- **Benedict Evans, Nov 2025** — *"The memory feature is a personal graph, not a network effect."* *"Whoever owns the union graph wins."*
- **Sutton & Silver, "Era of Experience"** — the next phase is AI *"learn[ing] directly from their interactions with the world"* via continual deep learning. The user's *episodic experience stream* becomes the substrate of personal agency.
- **Bessemer** — vertical AI 10× legacy SaaS; defensibility = domain data + workflow integration.
- **Korinek "moderate AGI" scenario** — 10–20 year transition window in which the inherited graph compounds value across decades.

Model commoditization is decisive. Repeated 2025–2026 refrain across builders: *"the foundational algorithm itself is no longer the moat; rather, it is the infrastructure, proprietary data, and deeply embedded workflows."*

### What we ship

1. **Continual learning over the graph.** Move from "agent reads the graph" to "agent shapes the graph." Sutton "Era of Experience" applied to one person.
2. **Multi-graph: team, family, intergenerational.** A user has a personal graph; teams and families have shared overlays. Mozilla.ai (Dickerson): *"true AI sovereignty must encompass control and agency across all levels — from nations down to individuals."* Overlay primitives allow sharing without surrendering.
3. **Voice / ambient ingest as a graph writer.** Meta's Dec 2025 Limitless acquisition signals incumbents own the hardware. Rowboat's posture: be the *graph these devices write into*, not another pendant. Clean ingestion API for any always-listening device that respects user-owned storage.
4. **Identity layer for agent-to-agent delegation.** Per a16z: *"the bottleneck for the agent economy is now identity, not intelligence."* Rowboat as the user's primary delegating agent needs verifiable identity to present to other agents.
5. **Style-mirror / voice-matching from the user's own past Markdown.** Cluster 8 complaint (Superhuman "obviously artificial text") is structurally addressed: every Rowboat-drafted output is conditioned on the user's own prior prose. Niche gripe for ops; kill-shot for the EA / founder / consultant ICPs.

### Customer-pain map

- Solves directly: cluster 8 (voice-matching).
- Honest absorption: cluster 2 (hallucinations) — every claim in the graph has a source file; the answer is provenance UX, not pretending the model doesn't hallucinate.

### Kill-shots to watch

- **Apple / Google bind personal context to hardware attestation.** Third-party graphs become second-class citizens — can't be background-active, can't access on-device models, can't surface in system search. Defense: deep desktop integration now, while the door is open; cultivate developer / power-user communities OS vendors hesitate to alienate.
- **A "personal Copilot graph" from Microsoft that's truly portable.** Unlikely but disruptive. Defense: ship better portability earlier — Markdown, git-native, exportable.
- **OpenAI/Anthropic ships native MCP-host enterprise SKUs with audit logs + SCIM.** Collapses the middle of the stack. Defense: be where the *graph* lives, not where the chat lives.

## Horizon 4 — Embodiment & Ambient (5–10 years, 2031–2036)

### What we believe

The personal AI escapes the laptop.

- **Hassabis (Lex Fridman / YC)** — *"a truly useful assistant that can come around with you in your daily life, not just stuck on your computer or one device,"* requiring world physics, memory, consistency, and continual learning. AGI ~2030 ±1.
- **Ben Thompson** — *"Glasses are going to be the best form factor for delivering AI because… you can let your AI assistant see what you see and hear what you hear and talk in your ear throughout the day."*
- **Azeem Azhar, "AI in 2030"** — your *"main interface will be a device like a smartphone, which will host your personal AI agent."*
- **Amodei "Machines of Loving Grace"** — *"50 to 100 years of biological and medical progress into 5 to 10 years"* if alignment holds. The work-counterpart claim: continuity of context over a decade compresses years of professional development.

### What we ship

1. **Embodied delegation.** The user's graph is what the robot vacuum / home robot / autonomous car *consults* to act on the user's behalf. Rowboat doesn't build robots; Rowboat is the personal context layer they query.
2. **Ambient ingestion at scale.** Hours-per-day voice, ambient screen, and wearable streams write to the graph continuously. The graph becomes the user's *episodic memory* literally, not metaphorically.
3. **Cross-decade longitudinal queries.** *"When did I first meet this person?"* *"What did I commit to in this relationship five years ago?"* *"What patterns recur in my work that I haven't noticed?"* These require a graph continuously, faithfully maintained — Rowboat's first-mover advantage in *graph longevity* matures into a defensible position.

### Counter-narratives this horizon must absorb

The strongest counter is that **everything stays in walled gardens**. Apple Intelligence + Meta glasses + Microsoft Copilot collectively own the consumer surface, and most users never adopt a third-party personal AI graph. *"Your phone knows more about you than any other device"* (Ben Thompson).

The **a16z "Year of Me" thesis** (Big Ideas 2026) is the most serious adjacent threat: argues 2026 is *"the moment when products stop being mass-produced and start being made for you,"* with personal context captured by the assistant the user lives in (ChatGPT, Apple mini-apps, OpenAI Apps SDK). If a16z is right, the graph accrues to whoever owns the *interface*, not to a separable personal-graph layer.

The Rowboat answer through this horizon: be the *professional* graph, the *multi-employer* graph, the *export-compatible* graph that survives the user switching ecosystems. Don't fight Apple for the median consumer; serve the operator whose professional context outlives any single employer, device, or assistant.

Tristan Harris's manipulation critique compounds the case: *"frictionless intelligence can produce frictionless influence."* A user-owned, local-first, auditable graph is the structural answer to the manipulation surface that incumbent-owned graphs create.

## Horizon 5 — Constitutional Sovereignty (10–20 years, 2036–2046)

> Sources for this horizon are thinner and more interpretive. Most claims are marked **[speculative]** — they extrapolate from credible directional voices rather than rest on direct quotes.

### What we believe

**1. Intergenerational AI memory becomes a recognized category. [speculative, directionally supported]** Combining Altman's *"your life just keeps appending to the context"* with PKG-grounded personal AI research (EpisTwin et al.), personal graphs become *inheritable* artifacts. Vannevar Bush's 1945 Memex lands as ordinary infrastructure.

**2. Agent-to-agent commerce becomes the primary transaction surface. [speculative, well-supported directionally]** Extrapolating Sequoia / a16z / Foundation Capital "agentic web": by ~2040 most micro-transactions, scheduling, procurement, and credentialing flow agent-to-agent. **3. "Right to a personal AI graph not owned by an employer or platform" becomes a labor and civil-rights claim. [speculative]** EU AI Act → US state laws → Mozilla / EFF framing. Analogous to medical-records portability today. Rowboat's local-first, exportable Markdown posture aligns with this trajectory by default.

### What we ship

The roadmap thins out honestly here. Product moves through this horizon are dictated less by what we plan now and more by what survives the previous horizons. What we can name:

1. **Graph inheritance and succession protocols.** Personal graph transferred to estate, successor at work, family member — with cryptographic continuity and provenance integrity.
2. **Multi-decade longitudinal analytics.** Tooling for the user to ask questions about their own life no one has been able to ask before.
3. **Public-interest infrastructure posture.** As personal AI becomes constitutional-grade infrastructure, the product's open-source posture, audit trail, and exit-portability become its license to operate.

## P0 defensive gaps (the must-fix list before scaling)

The customer-complaint research surfaced three product gaps the previous roadmap missed entirely. These are not horizons — they are pre-scale must-haves:

1. **Observability per `live:` note.** Run history, per-run cost, inline failure alerts, "this trigger has been silent for N days" warnings, kill-switch. Without this, every live note is a potential Lindy "agent vanished" / Replit "deleted prod DB" / LangChain "$47K loop" story. **Ship before any growth push.**
2. **BYO-model cost transparency.** Cursor July 2025 and Claude Code March 2026 prove that even pro users revolt over surprise meter drain. A BYO-model product offloads cost to the user *but inherits the blame.* Per-live-note token budgets, soft caps, "this run cost $X" inline display, cost-trend dashboards. **Ship as part of Horizon 1.**
3. **Voice-matching / style-mirror.** Rowboat's `live:` output is writing. Every draft conditioned on the user's prior prose. Listed as Horizon 3 above, but the version-0 implementation (light style adaptation from the user's sent-email corpus) belongs in Horizon 1 — it is the cluster-8 ("sounds like AI") differentiator from day one.

Secondary defensive gaps:

4. **Provenance UX for cluster-2 (hallucination).** Every claim in the graph displays its source file, last-updated timestamp, and ingestion path. The answer to "the AI made something up" is *"open the source — it's the note your meeting wrote."* Lessens hallucination harm without pretending it doesn't happen.
5. **A "this was generated, not asserted" mode** for live-note output that lands in user-facing surfaces (drafted emails, slide content). Moffatt v. Air Canada is now baked into enterprise procurement — the disclaimer surface is structural, not optional.

## Pricing model evolution

The roadmap should plan for the pricing transition consulting research has surfaced:

- **2026** — single-seat pro pricing: $30–80/mo for founder/EA, lifetime license option for the principled-niche prosumer (Msty's lifetime-license model showed this audience exists). Free tier for Obsidian-fluent beachhead.
- **2027–2028** — hybrid seat + credits, mirroring GitHub Copilot's June 2026 usage-based switch and Cursor's June 2025 credit migration. Vertical-professional tier at $100–300/mo (Harvey's $1,200/lawyer/mo is the upper bound on what professional fiduciary segments will pay).
- **2029+** — platform consumption-based pricing for embedded use cases (Sierra-style: per-resolution / per-action, not per-seat). Enterprise self-hosted Helm-chart deployment with annual contract, matching LangSmith's enterprise motion.

**Pricing model anti-patterns to avoid:**

- *Pure usage* — procurement kills it (unpredictable bills, Cursor's July 2025 backlash).
- *Pure seat* — power-user economics kill it (Cursor / Claude Code rate-limit complaints).
- *Hidden credits* — Lindy / Cursor complaints show users revolt fast when meter is opaque.

## Product portfolio strategy (Model B)

Rowboat ships alongside two other Oppulence Engineering products: **Cadence** (AI-native accounts payable automation) and **Canvas** (B2B revenue operations OS). All three share the same internal workflow + agent + MCP + sovereign-data runtime — `apps/rowboat` is the canonical implementation — but are positioned, sold, and marketed independently.

This roadmap is Rowboat's roadmap. Cadence and Canvas have their own. The portfolio strategy below describes how the three relate.

### The model: shared infrastructure, separate brands

The architectural pattern across all three products is identical: DAG-based workflow + BYO LLM + MCP-native integrations + sovereign-data option. That is real engineering efficiency — one platform investment, three vertical surfaces. The discipline of Model B is exploiting that efficiency *internally* while keeping the three products *externally* positioned for their distinct buyers.

The rule: **Rowboat's customer never has to think about Cadence or Canvas to buy Rowboat.** Cadence's customer never has to think about Rowboat to buy Cadence. Only investors, partners, and employees see the shared substrate.

Why not co-market? Three reasons grounded in the customer-pain and consulting research above:

1. **Three different buyers at three different price points.** Rowboat founder/EA at $30–80/mo single-seat ≠ Cadence CFO with 50+ invoices/month ≠ Canvas firm-wide RevOps buyer. Bundling means no single buyer gets a clean pitch.
2. **Thesis dilution.** Rowboat's load-bearing claim ("the asset is the user's graph, not the model") is sharp. Bundling with AP/AR automation muddies it.
3. **Sales motion conflict.** Three different motions (PLG-prosumer, CFO top-down, firm-operator mid-touch) = three different teams = burn that kills before any wedge proves out.

### Public visibility hierarchy

| Layer | Rowboat | Cadence | Canvas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing emphasis | Megaphone — site, launch, content, press | Quiet — site exists, no launch | Same |
| Product-specific landing page | Fully built out | Simple, "private beta" or design-partner gated | Same |
| Mentioned in Rowboat marketing | n/a | **No.** Not on one-pager, not in pitch, not on site. | Same |
| GitHub presence | Public, active | Public, active | Public, active |
| Mentioned when asked | "Flagship product" | Honest one-line: *"AI-native AP, currently with a small design-partner cohort."* | Same |
| Shared runtime story told publicly | Yes, as Rowboat Platform — *"the same runtime powers our other internal AI products."* | n/a externally | n/a externally |

The discipline is **asymmetric public emphasis**: one product gets the megaphone, the others are honestly visible but quietly maintained until they earn their own megaphone. Stealth signals weakness; quiet signals focus.

### Trigger conditions for promoting Cadence or Canvas to megaphone

Each product gets its own public launch only when it independently hits all four:

1. **5+ design partners actively using it in production**, not just signed contracts.
2. **A clear ICP** the team can describe in one sentence without hedging.
3. **At least one renewal cycle showing retention** — the only honest PMF signal.
4. **A repeatable sales motion** — two deals closed via the same playbook.

Until then each ships to private cohorts. A year without hitting these triggers is a sunset signal, not a "wait longer" signal.

### How this interacts with the horizons

- **Horizon 1 (2026)** — Rowboat is the public product. Cadence and Canvas remain in private beta with design partners. The shared runtime (`apps/rowboat`) is the engineering substrate for all three, but only Rowboat Desktop is publicly launched.
- **Horizon 2 (2027–2028)** — If Cadence or Canvas independently hits the trigger conditions above, it gets its own public launch with its own positioning. The platform/runtime story can be told publicly as Rowboat Platform — *"the same runtime powers our other internal AI products"* — without enumerating them.
- **Horizon 3+ (2028+)** — If two or more of the three have proven wedges, the "AI-native back office for professional services firms" suite framing becomes available. Until then, explicitly defer it. The Model B → Model C transition is earned, not declared.

### Anti-patterns to avoid

- **Bundling rumors.** If any investor, journalist, or customer characterizes the company as "the AI back-office suite," correct it immediately: *"Three products on shared infrastructure, sold separately."* That sentence kills the suite framing before it sticks.
- **Cross-product CTAs.** No "users of Rowboat also love Cadence" emails. No Cadence callouts in the Rowboat onboarding. Every cross-product mention erodes focus.
- **Stealth on the secondary products.** The GitHub org is public; the repos are visible. Denying the other products exists is untenable and creates suspicion. The discipline is *quiet*, not *secret*.

### Portfolio-relevant kill-shots (added to watchlist)

Two developments to track in addition to the Rowboat-specific kill-shots below:

- **A competitor ships an integrated AI-native back-office suite** (Karbon, Ramp, Bill.com, or a vertical AI startup) at the SMB / professional-services mid-market. Would compress the eventual Model C window. Defense: ensure each vertical product has its own moat independent of any suite story.
- **A workflow / agent runtime competitor** (LangChain, Mastra, Vercel AI SDK) ships a vertical AI app on its own platform. Validates the runtime-as-business thesis but races for category ownership of the underlying substrate. Defense: keep the Rowboat Platform investment ahead on the dimensions the verticals actually need (sovereign data, MCP-native, graph-as-substrate).

## The single load-bearing claim

If one sentence has to be right for everything else to follow:

> **The asset is the user's graph, not the model.**

Endorsed across an intentionally diverse set of voices: Foundation Capital ("Context Graphs"), Karpathy ("agentic wiki"), Evans ("memory is a personal graph, not a network effect"), Bessemer ("vertical AI 10× SaaS — defensibility is domain data + workflow integration"), Kambhampati ("LLMs Can't Plan" — agents need external scaffolding), Lakhani ("jagged frontier" — context-fit determines value), Sutton ("Era of Experience" — episodic stream as continual-learning substrate), Azhar ("personal AI as digital Chief of Staff by 2030"), and Altman ("your life just keeps appending to the context").

The strongest *competing* thesis is **a16z's "Year of Me"** — that the graph accrues to whichever interface (ChatGPT, Apple mini-apps, OpenAI Apps SDK) the user lives in. Rowboat's response: serve the multi-employer, multi-decade, professional-context user whose graph cannot be captured by any single interface vendor.

We take this bet because nine independent voices across academia, journalism, venture, and industry converge on the architecture; because the architecture follows from first principles (models commoditize, weights are not portable across vendors, what the user accumulates *about themselves* across years cannot be reconstructed from any single product's logs); and because the consulting / analyst layer has now translated it into a CIO-buyable category (*sovereign AI*) with Deloitte's 83% and McKinsey's 95% behind it.

The graph is the only artifact that compounds.
