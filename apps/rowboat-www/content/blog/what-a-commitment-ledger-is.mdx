A CRM is good at stages and amounts. An inbox is good at threads. A meeting tool is good at transcripts. None of them is a longitudinal record of what was promised.

Oppulence keeps that missing record: what your team owes, what the other side owes, each row tied to the email, meeting, or CRM object it came from.

That is not a second pipeline. The deal stage stays in HubSpot if you use HubSpot. The sentence that closed the stage belongs on the register.

## Why this is a separate object

Promises are created in conversation and kept by someone else. They are only noticed when they break. Putting them on a sticky note or a task list loses the source. Putting them only in the thread that created them means nobody finds them three weeks later.

The register has a direction, a lifecycle, and a link back to the source. If the source is missing, the row says so.

## What we will not claim

We will not tell you this replaces your CRM. We will not tell you the desktop app is "local-only" if you sign in. We will not invent a customer wall to make the category look older than it is.
